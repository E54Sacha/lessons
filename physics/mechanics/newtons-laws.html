🍎Newton's laws of motion

Three simple rules that explain how forces make things start, stop, speed up and push back.

beginnerphysics2026-11-26

💡The idea

A force is a push or a pull. Newton's three laws describe what forces do to objects. The first law: nothing changes its motion unless a force makes it. The second law: a bigger force gives a bigger change, and a heavier object changes less. The third law: when you push something, it pushes you back just as hard.

💡 Think of it like this

A shopping cart stays still until you push it (law 1). An empty cart speeds up faster than a full one (law 2). And you feel the cart's handle pressing back into your hands (law 3).

📖Key words

Force
A push or a pull, measured in newtons (N).
Net force
The total of all forces on an object, with opposite directions subtracting.
Mass
How much "stuff" an object contains, in kilograms (kg).
Acceleration
How quickly the speed (or direction) changes, in metres per second squared (m/s²).
Inertia
An object's tendency to keep doing what it is already doing.

⚙️How it works

  1. Law 1 (inertia): if the net force is zero, a still object stays still and a moving object keeps moving at the same speed in a straight line.
  2. Find the net force: add forces in the same direction, subtract forces in opposite directions.
  3. Law 2: if the net force is not zero, the object accelerates: \( F = m \times a \), so \( a = F / m \).
  4. Law 3: forces come in pairs. If A pushes B, B pushes A with the same size of force, in the opposite direction.
⚠️ Common mistake

The two forces of law 3 do not cancel out, because they act on different objects. You push the cart, the cart pushes you.

🔍Example

You push a 20 kg shopping cart with 50 N. Friction from the floor pulls back with 10 N. How fast does the cart speed up?

Step 1: net force

$$F_{\text{net}} = 50\ \text{N} - 10\ \text{N} = 40\ \text{N}$$

Step 2: law 2

$$a = \frac{F_{\text{net}}}{m} = \frac{40\ \text{N}}{20\ \text{kg}} = 2\ \text{m/s}^2$$

The cart gains 2 m/s of speed every second. Now fill the cart so it weighs 40 kg: the same 40 N gives only \( a = 40 / 40 = 1\ \text{m/s}^2 \). More mass, less acceleration.

Step 3: law 3

While you push the cart with 50 N, the cart pushes your hands back with 50 N.

Step 4: law 1

If you push with exactly 10 N, the forces balance (net force 0). A moving cart then keeps rolling at a steady speed; it does not slow down.

⚖️Why use it

✅ Advantages

  • Predicts the motion of almost everything around you: cars, balls, rockets, bridges.
  • Only one simple formula, \( F = m \times a \), to compute things.

⚠️ Limits

  • Stops being exact for objects near the speed of light or for tiny particles like atoms.

🔀Other ways

Energy methods: track kinetic and potential energy instead of forces, often easier when paths are curved.
Relativity and quantum mechanics: needed for very fast or very small objects, where Newton's laws break down.

🎯Quiz

  1. An object keeps doing what it is doing unless a _____ acts on it.

    Answer

    Force. This is the first law: without a (net) force, motion does not change.

  2. Is it true that a moving object needs a constant force to keep moving?

    Answer

    No, false. With zero net force, a moving object keeps moving forever. Things on Earth slow down only because friction is a force acting against them.

  3. The tendency of an object to keep doing what it is already doing is called _____.

    Answer

    Inertia. Inertia is why passengers lurch forward when a bus brakes: their bodies keep moving.

  4. Force is measured in a unit called the _____.

    Answer

    Newton. One newton (N) is the force that gives 1 kg an acceleration of 1 m/s².

  5. What is the net force on an object?

    1. The biggest force acting on it
    2. The total of all forces, with opposite directions subtracting
    3. Only the force you push with
    4. The object's weight
    Answer

    B. The net force combines every force, taking direction into account. A is tempting, but smaller forces like friction also count.

  6. Is it true that an object can be moving even when the forces on it are balanced?

    Answer

    Yes, true. Balanced forces mean no change in motion, so a moving object keeps moving at a steady speed in a straight line.

  7. If you double the net force on the same object, what happens to its acceleration?

    1. It is halved
    2. It stays the same
    3. It doubles
    4. It becomes four times bigger
    Answer

    C. In \( a = F / m \), acceleration grows in proportion to force. A is the mistake of mixing up force and mass.

  8. A net force of 10 N acts on a 2 kg object. What is its acceleration?

    1. 20 m/s²
    2. 12 m/s²
    3. 0.2 m/s²
    4. 5 m/s²
    Answer

    D. \( a = F / m = 10 / 2 = 5\ \text{m/s}^2 \). A is tempting, but it multiplies instead of dividing.

  9. Why does a heavy object speed up more slowly than a light one when pushed with the same force?

    Answer

    Because \( a = F / m \): with the same force, a bigger mass gives a smaller acceleration. A full cart speeds up slower than an empty one.

  10. What does the third law say happens when you push on a wall?

    Answer

    The wall pushes back on you with a force of the same size in the opposite direction. That is why you feel the wall pressing on your hands.

  11. Is it true that the two forces of a third-law pair act on the same object?

    Answer

    No, false. They act on two different objects: you push the cart, and the cart pushes you.

  12. Why don't the two forces of a third-law pair cancel each other out?

    Answer

    Forces only cancel when they act on the same object. A third-law pair acts on two different objects, so each object feels its own force and can still move.
