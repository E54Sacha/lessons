🧮Matrices

A grid of numbers you can add and multiply, like one big number.

beginnermaths2026-11-26

💡The idea

A matrix is a table of numbers arranged in rows and columns, like a small spreadsheet. It lets you store many related numbers together and work on all of them at once.

Matrices are used to solve systems of equations, move and rotate shapes in video games, and store data such as images. Once you know the rules, you can treat a whole table almost like a single number.

📖Key words

Matrix
A rectangular grid of numbers, written inside big brackets.
Entry
One single number inside the matrix (also called an element).
Size
Rows × columns: a 2 × 3 matrix has 2 rows and 3 columns.
Matrix addition
Adding two matrices of the same size, entry by entry.
Matrix multiplication
Combining two matrices by pairing a row of the first with a column of the second.

⚙️How it works

  1. Write the numbers in a grid. Rows go left to right, columns go top to bottom.
  2. Name its size: rows first, then columns. Always in that order.
  3. To add two matrices, they must have the same size. Add the entries that sit in the same place.
  4. To multiply a matrix by a plain number, multiply every entry by that number.
  5. To multiply A × B, the columns of A must equal the rows of B. Each entry of the result is one row of A "times" one column of B: multiply the pairs and add them up.
  6. Order matters: A × B is usually not the same as B × A.
✅ Tip

Write the sizes side by side: 2 × 3 and 3 × 4. If the inner numbers match, you can multiply, and the outer numbers (2 × 4) give the size of the result.

🔍Example

Take two 2 × 2 matrices:

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix} \qquad B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 4 \\ 2 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$$

Addition: same place, add

$$A + B = \begin{pmatrix} 2+1 & 1+4 \\ 0+2 & 3+5 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 5 \\ 2 & 8 \end{pmatrix}$$

Multiplication: row times column

$$A \times B = \begin{pmatrix} 2{\cdot}1 + 1{\cdot}2 & 2{\cdot}4 + 1{\cdot}5 \\ 0{\cdot}1 + 3{\cdot}2 & 0{\cdot}4 + 3{\cdot}5 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 13 \\ 6 & 15 \end{pmatrix}$$

Now compute B × A the same way:

$$B \times A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 13 \\ 4 & 17 \end{pmatrix} \neq A \times B$$
⚠️ Common mistake

With normal numbers, 2 × 3 = 3 × 2. With matrices, swapping the order usually changes the answer, as you can see above.

⚖️Why use it

✅ Advantages

  • Stores many numbers in one neat object and handles them all with one operation.
  • Turns big systems of equations into one short line that computers solve fast.

⚠️ Limits

  • Multiplication is less intuitive than for normal numbers: sizes must match and order matters.

🔀Other ways

Writing equations one by one: fine for 2 or 3 small equations, but it gets messy quickly as they grow.
Vectors: a single row or column of numbers, enough when you only need a list rather than a full table.

🎯Quiz

  1. A matrix is a rectangular grid of numbers arranged in rows and _____.

    Answer

    Columns. A matrix is organised in rows (across) and columns (down).

  2. A matrix has 2 rows and 3 columns. How do we write its size?

    1. 3 × 2
    2. 2 × 3
    3. 6
    4. 2 + 3
    Answer

    B. The size is always written rows first, then columns. A is the tempting mistake of putting columns first.

  3. Each single number inside a matrix is called an _____.

    Answer

    Entry. An entry (or element) is one number at one position in the grid.

  4. Is it true that you can add any two matrices, even if their sizes are different?

    Answer

    No, false. Addition is done entry by entry, so both matrices need exactly the same number of rows and columns.

  5. How do you add two matrices of the same size?

    Answer

    You add the entries that are in the same position. For example, the top-left of the result is the top-left of the first plus the top-left of the second.

  6. What happens when you multiply a matrix by the number 3?

    1. Only the first row is multiplied by 3
    2. The matrix gets 3 more rows
    3. Every entry is multiplied by 3
    4. Only the numbers on the diagonal are multiplied by 3
    Answer

    C. Multiplying by a plain number scales every entry. A is tempting, but the rule applies to the whole matrix, not one row.

  7. How do you find one entry of the product of two matrices?

    Answer

    You take one row of the first matrix and one column of the second, multiply the numbers pair by pair, and add the results.

  8. Is it true that to multiply A by B, the number of columns of A must equal the number of rows of B?

    Answer

    Yes, true. Each row of A is paired with each column of B, so they must contain the same number of values.

  9. A is a 2 × 3 matrix and B is a 3 × 4 matrix. What is the size of A × B?

    1. 3 × 3
    2. 2 × 3
    3. 4 × 2
    4. 2 × 4
    Answer

    D. The inner numbers (3 and 3) must match, and the outer numbers (2 and 4) give the size of the result. A is tempting, but 3 × 3 uses the inner numbers instead of the outer ones.

  10. Is it true that A × B always gives the same result as B × A?

    Answer

    No, false. Matrix multiplication usually depends on order; in the example, A × B and B × A gave different matrices.

  11. Why are matrices useful?

    Answer

    They hold many related numbers in one object and let you work on all of them at once, which helps solve systems of equations, move shapes in graphics, and store data.
